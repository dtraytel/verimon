# VeriMon efficiency study: refinement hypothesis and verified code optimizations

This document records an investigation into why VeriMon (the verified kernel, `-verified`)
is slower than MonPoly (the unverified kernel sharing the same binary), a quantitative test
of the hypothesis that refining the formalization to Imperative HOL or LLVM would close the
gap, and the verified optimizations implemented on this branch (`refine`).

All measurements were taken on the same machine (4 cores, OCaml 4.14.1 without flambda,
`dune build --profile=release`), single-threaded, wall-clock, best of one run (noise < 5%).

## 1. Baseline: where does VeriMon spend its time?

### 1.1 Benchmarks

* `P2`, `P3`: the CAV'10 policies shipped in `examples/mfotl`, with logs produced by
  `tools/gen_log` (`-event_rate 2000 -time_span 100`, about 200k events).
* Synthetic formulas over the signature `A(int,int) B(int,int) C(int)` with a log of 2000
  time points, 50 `A`- and 50 `B`-tuples and 13 `C`-tuples per time point drawn from a domain
  of 200 values (`evaluation/synthetic/gen_log.py 2000 50 200 log.log`, formulas in the same directory):

  | name | formula |
  |---|---|
  | and | `A(x,y) AND B(y,z)` |
  | andnot | `A(x,y) AND NOT B(x,y)` |
  | once | `ONCE[0,20] A(x,y)` |
  | since | `B(x,y) SINCE[0,50] A(x,y)` |
  | eventually | `EVENTUALLY[0,10] A(x,y)` |
  | until | `A(x,y) UNTIL[0,20] B(x,y)` |
  | agg | `s <- SUM y; x A(x,y)` |
  | complex | `A(x,y) AND (ONCE[0,20] B(y,z)) AND NOT C(z)` |

Baseline timings (MonPoly vs. VeriMon before this branch; outputs are identical):

| formula | MonPoly | VeriMon (old) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2 | 0.44 s | 2.83 s | 6.4x |
| P3 | 0.45 s | 2.64 s | 5.9x |
| and | 0.17 s | 0.79 s | 4.6x |
| andnot | 0.18 s | 0.76 s | 4.2x |
| once | 0.78 s | 3.30 s | 4.2x |
| since | 0.29 s | 1.01 s | 3.5x |
| eventually | 0.49 s | 1.74 s | 3.6x |
| until | 0.26 s | 0.60 s | 2.3x |
| agg | 0.17 s | 0.49 s | 2.9x |
| complex | 0.99 s | 8.85 s | 8.9x |

Parsing the 2 MB synthetic log alone costs about 0.08 s (MonPoly) / 0.19 s (VeriMon, which
additionally converts every tuple into the verified `event_data option list` representation
and inserts it into an RBT-based database).

### 1.2 Profiles (`perf record`, `--call-graph dwarf`)

The profiles of the old VeriMon are flat: no single monitor operation dominates. Aggregated
over `P2`, `P3`, `and`, `complex`:

* 20-30% garbage collection (`do_some_marking`, `caml_oldify_*`, `bf_allocate`, ...),
  i.e. allocation pressure from persistent data structures and short-lived closures;
* 12-25% comparator dispatch: `comparator_of` (which performs *two* dictionary-dispatched
  comparisons, `<` and then `=`, per element), `comparator_list`, `comparator_option`,
  `comparator_event_data`, `compare_nat`, `caml_apply2` (unknown function calls through
  closures);
* 8-12% *construction* of type-class dictionaries at run time (`ccompare_lista`,
  `ccompare_optiona`, `ceq_lista`, `ceq_seta`, ...). Every polymorphic function generated by
  Isabelle's code generator rebuilds the `ccompare`/`ceq` records for `event_data option list`
  on every call, e.g. once per inserted tuple inside a fold;
* 10-25% red-black-tree operations proper (`rbt_comp_ins`, `balance`, `rbt_comp_lookup`);
* 3-5% arbitrary-precision arithmetic on `nat` (`Z.add`, `ml_z_compare`, `suc`, ...).

Inclusive attribution to monitor operations:

* `P2` (Until): 62% in `add_new_mmuaux` (the Until auxiliary structure), of which 26% in
  `eval_step_mmuaux`; the rest is buffering, database construction and output conversion.
* `P3` (Since): 56% in `meeval_since` (`add_new_ts_mmsaux` 20%, `shift_end` 14%,
  `gc_join_mmsaux` 10%, `add_new_table_mmsaux` 9%), 7% multi-way join.
* `and`, `complex` (joins): 58-65% in `New_max_getIJ_genericJoin`, the worst-case optimal
  multi-way join, which repeatedly projects and intersects tables (`projectTable`,
  `inf_seta`, `image`) and builds many intermediate RBT sets. The binary nested-loop join
  (`-no_mw`) is even slower on `complex` (21.6 s).

## 2. Testing the refinement hypothesis

The hypothesis was that refining the algorithm to Imperative HOL (mutable arrays, hash
tables) or to LLVM (via Isabelle-LLVM) would make VeriMon competitive.

### 2.1 A direct data point: MonPoly's Since/Until

MonPoly's `src/optimized_mtl.ml` is an *imperative* OCaml port of exactly VeriMon's
`mmsaux`/`mmuaux` algorithms (`Optimized_Since.thy`, `Optimized_Until.thy`), using
`Hashtbl` for the tuple-indexed mappings and mutable queues. On `P3`/`since`/`P2` it is
3.5-6x faster than the generated code. This upper-bounds what "the same algorithm,
imperative, hand-written" can gain, but it conflates several effects.

### 2.2 Decomposing the gap with a microbenchmark

`evaluation/microbench/micro.ml` (`dune build evaluation/microbench/micro.exe`) inserts 300k random binary tuples into a set
and then performs 300k membership tests, using the generated code and hand-written OCaml on
the *same* data:

| implementation | time |
|---|---|
| (a) generated `RBT_set` insert/member, dictionaries rebuilt per call (as in generated polymorphic code) | 1.81 s |
| (a') generated `RBT_set`, dictionaries built once | 1.49 s |
| (b) `Set.Make` (AVL) on `event_data option list` with a direct hand-written comparator | 0.85 s |
| (c) `Set.Make` on `Z.t list` with polymorphic compare (MonPoly's representation) | 0.80 s |
| (c') `Set.Make` on `int list` with polymorphic compare | 0.76 s |
| (d) `Hashtbl` on `event_data option list` | 0.36 s |
| generated `inf_set` (RBT intersection), 20 x 150k | 2.32 s |
| `Set.Make.inter`, 20 x 150k | 1.17 s |

Conclusions:

1. The tuple representation (`event_data option list` vs. `cst list`) is irrelevant
   ((b) vs. (c) vs. (c')).
2. Dictionary passing and generic comparator dispatch cost about **2x** ((a) vs. (b)):
   1.2x for rebuilding dictionaries, 1.7x for the comparator call chain
   `comparator_list -> comparator_option -> comparator_event_data -> comparator_of ->
   less/eq` through closures and dictionary records.
3. Persistent balanced trees vs. an ephemeral hash table cost another **2.3x** ((b) vs. (d)).
4. The product (about 5x) matches the observed gap for the Since/Until-dominated
   benchmarks. For join-dominated benchmarks the additional factor is algorithmic (Section 1.2).

### 2.3 Assessment of Imperative HOL and LLVM refinement

* **Imperative HOL** would address item 3 only. It does not remove dictionary passing: the
  Imperative HOL collections (hash maps in the Separation Logic / Refinement Framework
  libraries) are themselves generic in `hashable`/`equal` dictionaries and would generate the
  same kind of code, and the `Heap` monad is compiled to OCaml as thunks
  (`unit -> 'a`), adding a closure allocation per bind. The expected gain is therefore bounded
  by roughly 2x on set/map heavy operations (perhaps 1.5x end-to-end by Amdahl's law), for a
  very large proof effort: `'a table = 'a tuple set` is the interface of all optimized
  theories (`Optimized_Since/Until/Trigger/Agg*`, about 20k lines of proof) and the monitor
  state is a purely functional value that is threaded, copied (e.g. `MLet`/`MFrz`
  snapshots) and compared; every use site would need a refinement relation and a
  separation-logic invariant.
* **Isabelle-LLVM** would additionally require reimplementing all containers, strings,
  arbitrary-precision integers and doubles in LLVM with manual memory management proofs, and
  a foreign-function marshalling layer between the OCaml front end (parser, MonPoly
  interoperability, output) and the LLVM heap. The front end alone already accounts for
  20-30% of VeriMon's run time on these benchmarks, so the marshalling would likely eat a
  substantial part of the gain.

**Verdict:** the hypothesis is only partially supported. Imperative refinement would buy at
most a ~2x constant factor on data-structure operations, whereas the measurements show that
(i) an equal factor is lost to code-generation artefacts (dictionaries, comparators) and
(ii) the largest factors on realistic formulas are algorithmic (the generic multi-way join
and the quadratic nested-loop binary join). Both (i) and (ii) can be fixed while staying in
the pure, already verified setting, with orders of magnitude less effort. This is what the
rest of this branch does. Imperative refinement remains a sensible *follow-up* once the cheap
factors are gone.

## 3. Verified optimizations implemented (`thys/MFODL_Monitor_Devel/Code_Opt.thy`)

All changes are new *code equations* proven as unconditional theorems and registered with
`[code]`; the definitions used in the correctness proofs (`Correctness.thy`) are untouched,
and so are the proofs. The exported `src/verified.ml` was regenerated from Isabelle.

1. **Direct comparators.** Code equations for `compare :: nat/integer/string8` and for the
   derived `comparator_event_data` that perform a single `<`/`=` test on the concrete type
   instead of going through `comparator_of` and the `linorder` dictionary.
2. **Index-based binary join (probe join).** The general case of `bin_join` (neither column
   set contains the other) clusters the *smaller* table by the common columns (`cluster`, an
   RBT of RBT sets) and probes the index with every tuple of the larger table, inserting the
   joined tuples directly into the result (`probe_join`, built from `comp_fun_idem` folds so
   that it executes through Containers' `set_fold_cfi`). The code equation is unconditional
   because it checks, in linear time, that every tuple is defined on the join columns
   (`complete_on`); otherwise it falls back to the nested loop (this never happens for
   well-formed tables). Indexing only the smaller side matters: in a formula such as
   `A(x,y) AND ONCE[0,20] B(y,z)` the `ONCE` table is large and re-joined at every time point,
   so clustering it each time would cost more than the join itself.
3. **Nested-loop join as a fold.** The default code equation for `A ⋈ B` builds a set of
   singleton sets (which requires the expensive lexicographic comparator on *sets*) and then
   takes its union. A code equation evaluates it as a double fold inserting joined tuples
   directly (`join_True_fold_code`). This also speeds up the bucket joins of item 2.

Additionally, in `thys/MFODL_Monitor_Devel/Optimized_Join.thy`:

4. **Pairwise multi-way join.** `mmulti_join` (used for `MAnds`, i.e. all conjunctions after
   `convert_multiway`) is now *defined* as a left-deep sequence of binary joins followed by
   anti-joins for the negative conjuncts; the worst-case optimal generic join is kept as
   `mmulti_join_gen`. `mmulti_join_correct` is re-proven with the same statement plus the
   assumption that the negated conjuncts' columns are covered by the positive ones, which
   `qtable_mmulti_join` (`Correct_Bin_Ops.thy`) already had (`fv_subset`); this is the only
   change to the correctness development (one line).

   The worst-case optimal generic join is still available: `mmulti_join` dispatches on the
   unspecified constant `use_generic_join` between `mmulti_join_gen` and the pairwise
   `mmulti_join_pw`, and `mmulti_join_correct` is proved for both values. The generated code
   reads the constant from the mutable flag `Verified.Config.generic_join` (a small
   `code_printing` module), which the front end sets from the new option `-generic_join`.
   The existing `-no_mw` option (do not flatten conjunctions into `MAnds` at all) is
   unchanged. Pairwise evaluation is the default because it was faster on all benchmarks
   here; the generic join keeps its worst-case guarantees on cyclic queries (see the
   `triangle` benchmark in Section 4).

   A first attempt kept the generic join as the definition and switched to the pairwise
   evaluation through an unconditional code equation guarded by a run-time check that all
   inputs are well-formed tables. That check is linear in the sizes of *all* input tables at
   every time point, while the join itself is often much cheaper (e.g. one new tuple against
   a large negated table): on `P3` it slowed VeriMon down by two orders of magnitude. Guards
   in code equations must be asymptotically dominated by the operation they guard; the
   `complete_on` check in `bin_join` (linear in the two tables that are being indexed) is fine.

## 5. Regenerating the verified kernel

```
cd thys && isabelle build -b -e -d . MFODL_Monitor_Devel      # about 35 min, needs ~8 GB RAM
cp MFODL_Monitor_Devel/verified.ocaml ../src/verified.ml
cd .. && dune build --profile=release && dune test
```

The AFP (Containers, Deriving, IEEE_Floating_Point, ...) must be registered as an Isabelle
component. `Code_Opt.thy`, `Imperative_Join.thy` and `Monomorphic_Code.thy` are the theories
between `Monitor_Impl.thy` and `Monitor_Code.thy`; the code equations they introduce are
checked like any other theorem, so the exported code remains verified.

## 6. Further opportunities (not implemented)

* **Dictionary hoisting / monomorphisation** was tried (see Section 7): the achievable gain
  is 10-15%, and an Isabelle-level formulation requires a data refinement of the tuple type
  through the whole implementation layer.
* **Native machine integers for `nat`.** Time points and time stamps are `Z.t` big integers.
  Mapping them to 63-bit OCaml integers would be unsound without overflow checks.
* **Until auxiliary structure.** `add_new_mmuaux` dominates Until formulas; it maintains
  three nested `Mapping`s keyed by tuples and rebuilds a `tmp` set per time point. An
  algorithmic review is warranted.
* **Front end.** For output-heavy formulas (`once`) more than a third of VeriMon's time is
  spent outside the kernel: converting result tables to MonPoly relations and printing them.
  The specialised `Tuple.compare` on this branch removes part of that; converting the
  verified RBT directly into a sorted list would remove the rest.
* **Imperative refinement** of `mmsaux`/`mmuaux` (Section 2.3), once the above are done.

## 7. Monomorphisation as a code-equation refinement (implemented)

**Goal.** 8-12% of VeriMon's time constructed `ceq`/`ccompare` dictionaries for
`event_data option list` at run time, and the generic comparator chain
(`comparator_list` -> `comparator_option` -> `comparator_event_data`) costs another
10-20% (Section 1.2). The generated code rebuilds a composite dictionary such as
`ccompare_list (ccompare_option ccompare_event_data)` at every call of a polymorphic function
that constructs it from its own dictionary parameters (`join1_insert`, `probe_step`,
`tmp_step`, ... are called once per tuple), and at every use of a container operation at a
tuple type in the monomorphic top-level code (1429 occurrences).

**Why a naive wrapper does not help.** A monomorphic wrapper constant such as
`ed_insert = (Set.insert :: event_data tuple => ...)` is eta-expanded by the ML serializer
(`let rec ed_insert x = insert (dicts) x`) and rebuilds the dictionaries on every call, and a
type copy `etuple` with a value dictionary (the design considered first) would require
re-deriving the whole implementation layer by transfer. Both were rejected.

**What is implemented (`thys/MFODL_Monitor_Devel/Monomorphic_Code.thy`, `monomorphize.ML`).**
A refinement at the level of code equations that touches no definition and no correctness
proof of the monitor, automated by a small Isabelle/ML tool:

1. *Boxing.* For a generic container operation `c` used at a closed type involving tuples of
   event data (`Set.insert`, `Set.member`, union, intersection, difference, `Set.filter`,
   `image`, `Ball`, `finite`, `card`, `set_eq`, `csorted_list_of_set`, the folds over
   red-black trees, `Mapping.lookup/update/delete/filter/keys/combine/is_empty` at the five
   value types occurring in the monitor state, `cluster`, `set_of_idx`, `mapping_join`,
   `mapping_antijoin`, `upd_set`, `upd_nested_max_tstp`, ...), a nullary constant
   `c_v = Box c` is defined. Its generated code is a *value* of a non-function type and hence
   not eta-expanded: `let ed_insert_v = Box (insert (ceq_list ..., ccompare_list ...))` builds
   the dictionaries once when the program starts. The `code_unfold` rule `c x = unbox c_v x`
   (proved from the definition) redirects all applied occurrences of `c` at that type; the
   applied form deliberately leaves `c` untouched where it is passed as an argument, e.g. as a
   locale parameter. Empty tables and empty mappings are the analogous nullary constants
   `ed_bot`, `ed_mempty_*`.
2. *Monomorphic copies.* For each polymorphic function `f` of the implementation that is hot
   at type `event_data` (the joins `join1_insert`, `join_row`, `probe_*`, `bin_join`,
   `mmulti_join*`, `proj_tuple_in_join*`, the Until candidate set `tmp_step`/`tmp_set`, the
   Since operations `join_mmsaux`, `add_new_table_mmsaux`, `add_new_ts_mmsaux`, `shift_end`,
   `gc_mmsaux`, the index operations `idx_*`, `update_mmtaux`, the regex and aggregation
   helpers, ...), a copy `ed_f = f` at `event_data` is defined. Its code equations are the
   (preprocessed) code equations of `f`, instantiated by the kernel to `event_data`, folded
   with the definition and simplified with `CCOMPARE(event_data tuple) = Some ed_comparator`
   and `CEQ(...) = Some ...`, so that the `case ID CCOMPARE(...) of None => abort | Some _ => ...`
   dispatch disappears. Inside `ed_f` all container operations are at closed tuple types and
   are redirected to the boxed values by (1); the rule `f x = ed_f x` redirects the callers.
3. *Folds without the `comp_fun_idem` wrapper.* Values of an abstract type (`comp_fun_idem`,
   `mapping_rbt`, `wf_idx`, ...) can only be built by the polymorphic abstract code equation
   of their constructor, which cannot be specialised. The fold-based code equations of
   Section 3 (`join_row`, `probe_step`, `probe_join`, `tmp_set`) therefore get a separate
   clause per set representation whose `RBT_set` case folds the step function directly over
   the tree (`RBT_Set2.fold (join1_insert a) rbt acc`), so that the monomorphic step is
   reached without passing through the wrapper.
4. *Compatibility.* The preprocessor rewrites bottom-up, so the redirections fire inside the
   left-hand sides of other `code_unfold` rules (`{a in A. P a}` is normally folded into
   `Set.filter P A`, `k in Mapping.keys m` into a lookup, `A = {}` into `Set.is_empty A`).
   These rules are restated for the redirected constants.

`ed_comparator` is a direct recursive comparator on `event_data tuple`, proved equal to the
derived `comparator_list (comparator_option comparator_event_data)`; it is what the
dictionary lemmas expose, but the red-black trees still call the derived comparator through
the boxed dictionary values, because the comparator inside a Containers dictionary cannot be
replaced per type instance.

**What remains polymorphic.** The Since structure stores its tables as `wf_table` /
`wf_idx`, whose operations (`wf_idx_join`, `wf_idx_antijoin`, `wf_idx_union`, ...) are
abstract code equations of a typedef with an invariant. Their bodies are generated once,
polymorphically, and call the polymorphic `idx_join` -> `bin_join` -> `probe_join` -> ...
chain, in which the per-tuple dictionary construction persists (148 occurrences in the
generated code, down from 202 constructions from parameters and from 236 closed
constructions; the remaining closed ones are the boxed values themselves and a few in the
aggregation operator). Removing them requires either making `wf_idx` a plain type with the
invariant carried by the proofs, or the `etuple` type copy.

**Effect.** Measured with the kernel exported from the session (`compare.sh`, same binary for
both kernels, distribution compiler): see the table "Monomorphisation" in Section 4. The gain
is 5-15% on the join- and Until-dominated formulas and within noise on the Since-dominated
ones. After this step the profile of P2 is
dominated by GC (about 30%), red-black-tree rebalancing and insertion (about 10%) and
comparisons of `nat` time stamps represented as `Z.t` (about 5%); dictionary construction
no longer appears among the top entries.

## 7b. Since structure: no tuple indices without join columns (implemented)

**Observation.** The Since structure (`mmsaux`, `Optimized_Since.thy`) keeps, next to the
result table `table_in` and the two mappings `tuple_in`/`tuple_since`, two indexed copies
`wf_table_in`/`wf_table_since` (`wf_table`, indexed by the join columns `args_L`). They are
read only by `join_mmsaux`, and only when there are join columns. For `ONCE`-like formulas
(`args_L = {}`: P3, `once`, the Since subformula of `complex`) every time point nevertheless
built an index for the moved, expired and new tables (`wf_idx_of_set`: filter, cluster,
`set_of_idx`), unioned indices (`wf_idx_union`) and antijoined them: about 28% of P3's time
for structures that were never read.

**Change (a small definitional refinement with re-proved invariants).** The four operations
`shift_end`, `add_new_ts_mmsaux'`, `add_new_table_mmsaux` and `gc_mmsaux` now compute the
indexed tables through `wf_table_upd args m (\<lambda>_. incremental)`: with join columns the
incremental index operation as before; without, `wf_table_of_keys n R m`, the well-formed
table whose set is the key set of the corresponding mapping (`wf_table_of_set n R (keys m)`).
The invariant `valid_mmsaux` is unchanged; its four preservation proofs only needed the two
facts `wf_table_sig_upd`/`wf_table_set_upd` (the set of keys of the new mapping is a table).
For the code generator, `wf_table_of_set` and `wf_table_of_keys` become representations of
`wf_table` (`code_datatype`, in `Code_Opt.thy`): constructing them is a constant-time
allocation, the reads (never executed without join columns) filter lazily, and the binary
operations convert the new representations to the plain set representation first, so
formulas with join columns run the same code as before. `wf_table_of_set_args` also no longer
builds an index for the empty join column set, and `init_mmsaux` starts from the plain
representation.

**Effect** (kernel exported from the session, two runs on an idle machine, distribution
compiler, compared with the monomorphised kernel of Section 7; all cram tests pass, all
outputs identical):

| formula | before | after (run 1 / run 2) | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| P3 | 1.84-1.90 s | 1.24 s / 1.24 s | 1.48-1.53x |
| once | 2.32-2.56 s | 1.92 s / 1.86 s | 1.25-1.33x |
| complex | 3.61-3.76 s | 3.00 s / 2.95 s | 1.20-1.27x |
| since (join columns, unchanged code path) | 0.81-0.84 s | 0.83 s / 0.84 s | 1.0x |
| P2, and, andnot, eventually, until, agg | | | unchanged (0.96-1.05x) |

P3 is now 2.5x slower than MonPoly (was 5.9x at the start of this branch); what remains in
its profile is the mapping maintenance itself (`upd_set_keys`, `filter_set`), the table
union/difference, the `valid_tuple` filter, GC and parsing. The same duplication exists for
formulas *with* join columns (index plus table plus mapping); removing it there needs the
index to become the only representation of `table_in`, a larger change to `Optimized_Since`.

**A pitfall worth recording.** Re-declaring `code_datatype` for `wf_table` silently drops all
existing code equations that pattern-match on its constructors; they must be re-declared
after it (`declare wf_table_union_code[code] ...`). The first build without this produced code
that failed with a pattern-matching exception for Since with join columns, caught by the
`since` benchmark and the test suite.


## 7c. Bulk construction of the database (implemented)

`insert_into_db` inserted every input tuple separately: a string-keyed mapping update and a
red-black-tree insertion per tuple (1.6% of P3, but 10-17% of `agg` and `since`, whose logs
have many tuples per time point relative to the kernel's work). Now the front end collects
the tuples of a time point per predicate, sorts them with an OCaml comparator that mirrors
the verified one, and hands the list to the verified `table_of_list`, which checks strict
sortedness with the verified comparator in linear time and then builds the tree in linear
time with `rbtreeify`; if the check fails (the two comparators disagree), it falls back to
repeated insertion, so the result is `set xs` in every case (`RBT_set_rbt_of_sorted_list`).
`insert_table_into_db` inserts the table with one mapping update and is proved equal to
folding `insert_into_db` over the list (`insert_table_into_db_fold`). Duplicates are removed
in the front end only when the tuples are equal as values (bitwise for floats), so no tuple
is lost. Effect: the database construction share of `agg` fell from about 25% to 17%; the
linear path is taken on all benchmark logs (`rbtreeify` 2.4%, sortedness check 1%).

## 7d. Aggregation without quadratic filtering; option-valued unions (implemented)

Profiling `agg` showed two more algorithmic problems, both fixed by proven code equations
in `Code_Opt.thy`:

* `eval_agg` filtered the whole table once per group and each group once per distinct value
  of the aggregated term. The new equation clusters the table by the group key and each
  group by the term value (`cluster`, one insertion per tuple), then applies the aggregation
  operator to the value/count pairs (`agg_values`).
* The `Match` mode of predicate evaluation computes `\<Union>v\<in>X. set_option (...)`, which
  Containers compiles through a set of sets whose comparator compares whole tables
  (`comp_of_ords`/`lexord_fusion`: 40% of the large aggregation run). The `code_unfold` rule
  `Union_set_option_these` rewrites it to `Option.these` of the image. This affects every
  predicate whose argument list is not a plain list of distinct variables, not only
  aggregations.

Effect (best of three, distribution compiler): `agg` (50 tuples per time point) 0.49 s ->
0.37 s; a log with 2000 tuples and 20 groups per time point 1.41 s -> 0.87 s (MonPoly:
0.24 s). With the regenerated canonical kernel (bulk construction, clustering, option-valued
unions; two runs, compared with the kernel of Section 7b): `agg` 0.52 s -> 0.42 s (1.24x), the
large aggregation log 1.47 s -> 0.91 s (1.6x; MonPoly 0.26 s), `and` 1.14x, `eventually`
1.08x, `until` 1.05x; P2, P3 and `since` within the run-to-run noise. All outputs identical,
all cram tests pass.

## 7e. Since structure: plain sets when all columns are join columns (implemented)

For `B(x,y) SINCE A(x,y)` and similar formulas the join columns of the Since structure are all
its columns (`args_L args = args_R args`), so the join in `join_mmsaux` is an intersection or a
set difference and the tuple indices (`wf_idx`, a mapping from projected keys to singleton
tables) are pure overhead: on the `since` benchmark 28% of the time went into building and
rebuilding them (`wf_idx_of_set`) and another 13% into flattening them (`set_of_idx`).

`join_mmsaux` and `join_mmasaux` get a third branch for this case: the deleted tuples are
`table_in - X` (or `table_in ∩ X` for negated left operands), `tuple_since` is filtered with
one membership test per key, and both `wf_table`s are maintained by `wf_table_upd`, whose
condition is extended to `args_L args = {} ∨ args_L args = args_R args`. The correctness
proof (`join_mmsaux_abs_eq`, then the aggregation variant) shows that the new branch
computes the same abstract structure as before (`join_eq` for the sets,
`proj_tuple_in_join_mask_idle` for the filter); the `wf_table` values agree by
`wf_table_eqI`. The code equation for `wf_table_of_set_args` picks the plain set
representation in the same case.

Effect (two runs, distribution compiler): `since` (`B(x,y) SINCE[0,50] A(x,y)`) 0.88-0.91 s ->
0.45-0.47 s (1.9x; MonPoly 0.30 s); all other benchmarks unchanged within noise (their Since
operators have no or a proper subset of join columns). `C(x) SINCE[0,50] A(x,y)`, where the
index is still needed, stays at 0.90 s (MonPoly 0.31 s): there the remaining time is spread
over the index construction for the new tables (`wf_idx_of_set`, 18%), the index antijoins
(16%) and flattening the deleted index (`set_of_idx`, 12%). All outputs identical, all cram
tests pass.

## 7f. Tuple projections without index arithmetic (implemented)

`restrict A v`, the projection of a tuple to a column set, is defined as
`map (λi. if i ∈ A then v ! i else None) [0..<length v]`: per tuple it allocates an index list
of big integers, tests set membership per position and accesses the list quadratically. It is
evaluated for every tuple of both operands of a probe join (as the key of the hash/tree
index) and for every tuple that enters a tuple index of the Since structure; in the `complex`
profile the index arithmetic (`upt`, `nth`, `length`, `Suc`) was about 15% of the time.

Two code equations in `Code_Opt.thy`/`Monomorphic_Code.thy`:

* `restrict A v = restrict_aux 0 A v` walks the tuple once (proved by `nth_equalityI`);
* the probe join receives the arity from `bin_join` (`probe_join_n n I t t' = probe_join I t t'`)
  and computes the key function once per join: `restrict_with I n` builds the mask
  `join_mask n I` and projects every tuple by a single traversal (`proj_tuple_chk`, which
  also checks the length so that `restrict_with I n = restrict I` holds unconditionally and
  no invariant is needed). `bin_join` also computes `join_mask` once per join instead of
  once per tuple in its subset cases (it was inside the filter predicate).
* The probe join's precondition that all join columns are defined in every tuple
  (`complete_on`, checked for every tuple of both operands on every join with `length` and
  `nth`, about 9% of `complex`) is checked by `complete_with`: the mask is built once per
  join and each tuple is checked in one traversal; it equals `complete_on` unconditionally.
  `complex` and `and` gain another 3-5% (scratch export).

Effect (scratch export, one run, distribution compiler): `complex` 3.28 s -> 2.86 s (1.15x),
`and` 1.09x, `andnot` 1.05x, `agg` 1.06x, `since` 1.04x, others within noise. With the
regenerated canonical kernel (compared with the kernel of Section 7e in one run; the front
end also carries the integer printer of Section 9.1): `complex` 3.40 s -> 2.87 s (1.18x),
`eventually` 1.22x, `until` 1.10x, `and` 1.09x, P2, P3, `andnot`, `once`, `agg` 1.05-1.06x.
All outputs identical, all cram tests pass.

## 7g. Until structure: one pass over the new table (implemented)

`add_new_mmuaux` built the pair set `tmp` (a tree with a pair comparator, one insertion per
new tuple), filtered it into a second tree, took its image for the table appended to the
queue, folded it into the nested mapping `a2_map` and filtered and imaged it once more for the
result. The new code equation (`Code_Opt.thy`, section "Code equations for the auxiliary
structures") computes the three consumers in a single fold over the operand: `until_step`
processes one pair (table insertion, nested update, result insertion when the pair belongs to
the expiring time point), `until_elem_step` the one or two pairs a tuple contributes. Both are
`comp_fun_idem`, so the fold over the operand equals the folds over the pair set
(`fold_until_elem_step`, `fold_until_step`, `until_fused`); the code equation is proved from
the definition of `add_new_mmuaux` and falls back to the original expression for infinite
tables. The aggregation variant `add_new_mmauaux` keeps the pair set, which it needs for its
own bookkeeping.

Effect (scratch export, one run): `eventually` 1.14 s -> 1.08 s and `until` 0.53 s -> 0.50 s
(1.06x); P2 unchanged within noise. The remaining Until time is in the tree operations that
are still necessary (the table and nested-mapping insertions, 45%) and in the collector
(30%). P2 in particular is a per-time-point workload: its log has 201157 time points with
on average one tuple each, and VeriMon spends about 11 us per time point against MonPoly's
2.5 us on the fixed work of a step (queue and mapping updates of the Until structure with
big-integer time points, buffers, the evaluation of the conjunction), not on tuples. Reducing
that needs a leaner representation of the Until state (e.g. no fresh inner mapping per time
point, machine-word time points), not another code equation. With the regenerated canonical
kernel: `eventually` 1.04x, `agg` 1.09x, all others within noise; all outputs identical, all
cram tests pass. The analogous fusion for the Since structure (moving the tables that enter
the window: filter by `valid_tuple`, update `tuple_in`, accumulate the new tuples, in one fold
per table instead of three passes) was proved and measured the same way and brought nothing
measurable on P3, `once` or `complex`, so it was not adopted: those passes run over the
one-tuple tables of the per-time-point logs, where the fixed cost per step dominates.

## 8. Imperative refinement of the join index (implemented, selectable)

`thys/MFODL_Monitor_Devel/Imperative_Join.thy` refines the index of the probe join to an
imperative hash table, using Imperative HOL and the separation-logic library
`Separation_Logic_Imperative_HOL` (its `Hash_Map`).

**Design.**
* `heap`, `countable` and `hashable` instances for `event_data` (and the underlying
  `double`, `string8`, `integer`, IEEE `float` types), so that tuples can be stored in the
  heap and hashed.
* `run :: 'a Heap ⇒ 'a` executes a heap computation on the empty heap and returns its
  result. `run_hoare` shows that a total-correctness triple `<emp> c <λr. ↑(r = v)>t` yields
  `run c = v`, i.e. the result of such a computation is a pure value independent of the heap.
  The generated OCaml applies the thunk (`(_ ())`); this and the standard Imperative HOL
  adaptation are the trusted parts.
* `probe_join_imp I xs ys` allocates a hash table, inserts the tuples of the smaller side
  keyed by their restriction to the join columns (`hm_insert_all`), probes it with the larger
  side (`probe_all`) and collects the joined tuples in a functional table. The Hoare triples
  `hm_insert_all_rule`, `probe_all_rule` and `probe_join_imp_rule` are proved with `sep_auto`
  by induction over the lists, against an abstract bucket map (`bucket_map`).
* `hprobe_join` wraps this with `run` and the list views of the tables; `hprobe_join_join`
  shows it equals the relational join under the same side conditions as `probe_join_join`.
  `bin_join_code_imp` is the new code equation for `bin_join`; it dispatches on the
  unspecified constant `use_imperative_join` (both branches proved), read from the flag
  `-imperative_join`.

**Generated-code pitfalls found on the way.** The first measurement was 60% *slower* than
the functional index. Profiling showed that 22% of the run time went into the conversions
between `integer` and `uint32` of `Native_Word`, whose default OCaml code tests bits one by one
and re-parses big-integer literals from strings on every call, and that the hash of string
literals materialises a character list. Both are now printed directly on machine integers and
bytes (documented in the theory). Another latent bug surfaced: `literal_string8` was printed
as `id`, which does not exist in OCaml's toplevel.

**Result.** With those fixes the imperative index is correct (all tests pass, outputs agree)
but still not faster than the persistent RBT index of Section 3:

| formula | VeriMon, RBT index (default) | VeriMon, `-imperative_join` |
|---|---|---|
| and | 0.40 s | 0.52 s |
| complex | 4.36 s | 4.79 s |
| other benchmarks | unchanged (no general-case binary join) | |

The profile of the imperative version attributes the difference to exactly the effects
anticipated in Section 2.3: the generic hash map code rebuilds the `heap` (`typerep`),
`hashable` and `equal` dictionaries on every operation (`heap_list`, `typerep_prod`,
`typerep_list` now appear in the profile), every monadic bind allocates a thunk, and array
indices go through arbitrary-precision `nat`. The asymptotic advantage of hashing over a
balanced tree is small here (the indexed side is the *smaller* table) and cannot compensate
these constant factors. The imperative join is therefore kept as an option for experiments
and as the template for imperative refinements of larger structures, whose payoff would be
subject to the same overheads.

## 9. Roadmap towards MonPoly's performance

Where the remaining time goes with the default kernel of this branch (inclusive shares,
`perf`, see Section 1.2 for the method):

| benchmark | kernel share | of which | front end share | of which |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P3 (Since) | 81% | Since structure 65% (`add_new_ts_mmsaux` 22%, `shift_end` 16%, `add_new_table` 11%, `gc_join` 11%) | 15% | parsing, database construction 3% |
| P2 (Until) | 88% | Until structure 67% (`eval_step_mmuaux` 29%) | 10% | parsing, database construction 2% |
| once | 39% | `add_new_table` 22%, `shift_end` 12% | 55% | output conversion 28%, printing 21%, database 7% |
| complex | 82% | joins 44%, Since 29% | 15% | printing 5%, output 4%, database 3% |

Inside the auxiliary structures no single pathological pattern is left (the set-of-sets
unions were removed from the joins; a small one remains in `add_new_mmuaux`): the time is
spread over red-black-tree insertions, deletions, filters, unions and intersections on
persistent tables and tuple-keyed mappings, index (re)construction for new tables
(`wf_idx_of_set`), and the constant factors identified in Section 2 (dictionaries,
comparators, GC, `nat` as big integers).

The plan, ordered by expected gain per effort:

1. **Front end (done on this branch, Section 9.1).** Convert result tables to lists instead
   of MonPoly relations, print through a buffer, format machine-size integers without
   `Z.to_string`, skip re-sorting when the kernel order already agrees. Output-heavy
   formulas gain 20-30%; the same printing path could be adopted by MonPoly.
2. **Database construction (done, Section 7c).** `insert_into_db` performed one RBT
   insertion and one string-keyed mapping update per input tuple (3-8%). The tuples of a
   time point are now accumulated per predicate on the OCaml side and each table is built
   by one verified bulk operation (sorted list to RBT, `rbtreeify`, linear time).
3. **Unions over sets of sets in the auxiliary structures (done, Section 9.2).**
   `add_new_mmuaux` built its candidate set as a union over an image of singleton sets, and
   `set_of_idx`, `gc_mmsaux` and the `eval_step` functions took unions of families of tables
   through a set of sets. Fold-based code equations gained 6-18% on P2, P3, since, once,
   eventually and until.
4. **Dictionary sharing / monomorphisation (done, Section 7).** Implemented as a
   code-equation refinement (boxed container operations, monomorphic copies of the hot
   functions). What is left is the polymorphic `wf_idx` chain of the Since structure
   (a typedef with an invariant, hence abstract code equations) and the aggregation
   operator; the direct tuple comparator is proved but cannot be injected into the
   Containers dictionaries. This is also the item that would make the imperative
   refinement (Section 8) pay off.
5. **Flambda (measured, Section 4).** The release Docker image builds with an flambda
   compiler; with it VeriMon gains another 10-30% while MonPoly is unchanged, so the
   distribution-compiler numbers overstate the gap by that much.
6. **Since/Until auxiliary structures.** The largest remaining share and the largest
   effort: their proofs (`Correct_Since`, `Correct_Until`, `Optimized_*`) are tied to the
   concrete data representation. First step done (Section 7b): for formulas without join
   columns the Since structure no longer maintains its tuple indices (P3 1.5x, once 1.3x,
   complex 1.2x); the same when all columns are join columns (Section 7e, since 1.9x).
   Next: (a) make the index the only representation of the result table for formulas with a
   proper subset of join columns and avoid rebuilding indices for every new table; (b) the
   corresponding duplications in the Until structure (`a1_map`/`a2_map`/`tables`); (c) an
   imperative refinement of `mmsaux`/`mmuaux` along the lines of Section 8, which only pays
   off after replacing `nat` indices by machine words in the hot paths.
7. **Machine integers for time stamps and time points** (3-5%): would require a
   verified bound or the `Native_Word` types throughout the monitor state; low priority.
8. **GC parameters (done: `-gc_space_overhead`).** The generated code allocates much more
   than MonPoly (P2: about 40% of the time in the collector and the best-fit allocator). A
   larger GC space overhead trades memory for speed: with 300 instead of the default 120,
   P2 1.11x, `once` 1.13x, P3 1.04x at about 1.3x the (small) heap; 400 gives P2 1.17x at
   1.4x the heap; a larger minor heap (`s=2M`-`16M`) is slower. The verified kernel now sets
   300 by default (MonPoly's own algorithm is unchanged) and the option
   `-gc_space_overhead N` overrides it in both modes; the trade-off scales with the live
   state, so a monitor with a large state can set 120 to keep MonPoly's memory behaviour.
   Aggressive `ocamlopt` inlining flags (`-O3 -inline 200 -unsafe`, without flambda) gain
   only 1-2%.

**The Until structure (P2, `eventually`, `until`; item 6b) in detail.** With the kernel of
Section 7f, P2 spends 55% in `add_new_mmuaux` and 19% in `eval_step_mmuaux`. Per new tuple
of the right operand, `add_new_mmuaux` builds the pair set `tmp` (a tree with a pair
comparator), filters it, takes its image twice (`table`, the `result` contribution) and folds
it into the nested mapping `a2_map` (an outer lookup plus an inner insertion): four to five
tree insertions per tuple where MonPoly performs one or two. `eval_step_mmuaux` merges the
inner mapping of the expiring time point into the next one (`Mapping.combine`) and rebuilds
the result set from its keys on every step, i.e. work proportional to the pending tuples per
time point. A fused single-pass code equation for `add_new_mmuaux` on the red-black-tree
representation (compute `table`, the `a2_map` update and the `result` contribution in one
fold over the operand, no pair set) is the next concrete step; it is a code-equation
refinement like Sections 7c-7f but needs the commutativity of the nested update for the
fold, so it is a day of proof work rather than an hour.

Matching MonPoly on the temporal benchmarks (P2/P3: still 4-5x) requires items 4 and 6;
the join-dominated formulas are already within 2-2.5x and item 2 plus 4 would bring them to
about 1.5x.

### 9.2 Code equations for the auxiliary structures (Code_Opt, section "Code equations for
the auxiliary structures")

* `tmp_set`: the candidate set of `add_new_mmuaux`/`add_new_mmauaux` as a `comp_fun_idem`
  fold (`tmp_step`) instead of `⋃ (f ` rel2)`;
* `set_of_idx`, `gc_mmsaux`, `eval_step_mmuaux`, `eval_step_mmauaux`: unions of a list of
  tables as `fold (∪)` instead of `⋃ (set xs)` / `⋃ (snd ` set xs)`.

All are unconditional code equations proved from the original ones; the correctness proofs
are untouched. Effect (idle machine, same run): P2 2.79 → 2.47 s, P3 2.43 → 2.30 s,
once 3.14 → 2.74 s, since 1.09 → 0.92 s, eventually 1.71 → 1.46 s, until 0.65 → 0.61 s.

### 9.1 Front end changes on this branch

* `Verified_adapter.step` returns the verdict tables in the kernel's representation;
  `Verified_adapter.show_results` folds them out of the red-black tree in kernel order,
  projects, checks sortedness with MonPoly's tuple order (re-sorting only if the
  free-variable permutation changed the order), and prints each time point through one
  buffer directly from `event_data` values, without building `Tuple.tuple`s or
  intermediate strings (a further 1.2x on `once`, 1.05-1.08x on P2 and `complex`). Machine-size
  integers are written into the buffer digit by digit instead of through `string_of_int`
  (which formats via `printf` and allocates): `once` 1.9 s -> 1.55 s (another 1.2x), `complex`
  1.04x. In the common case (no projection, not verbose) the tuples are streamed from the
  tree straight into the buffer with the order check done on the fly, so no tuple lists are
  built at all (`once` another 1.10x); `once` is now within 1.5x of MonPoly, whose output
  path is the same as before.
* `Predicate.string_of_cst` formats integers that fit a machine word with `string_of_int`.
* `Tuple.compare` is specialised (Section 4).

## 10. Imperative refinement of the Since structure: feasibility and potential

Asked to try an imperative refinement of the Since structure, this section records what was
tried, what blocks it, and what it would be worth. The measurements come from a
micro-benchmark that drives the auxiliary structures directly (`evaluation/since_bench/`):
200000 time points, time stamps advancing every 1700 time points as in the P2/P3 logs, one
tuple per time point ("dense") or one non-empty table every eight time points ("sparse", the
shape of P3's `auth` stream), against a hash-table sliding window doing the same work.

**What an imperative structure would gain.** Per step, ONCE with window [2,21):

| operation | dense | sparse |
|---|---|---|
| `shift_end` | 0.58 us | 0.32 us |
| `add_new_ts_mmsaux'` | 2.22 us | 0.71 us |
| `gc_join_mmsaux` | 0.68 us | 0.28 us |
| `add_new_table_mmsaux` | 0.57 us | 0.19 us |
| total | 4.1 us | 1.5 us |
| imperative window (hash table + queue) | 0.40 us | 0.40 us |

and EVENTUALLY[0,6) for the Until structure:

| operation | dense | sparse |
|---|---|---|
| `shift_mmuaux` (runs the `eval_step`s) | 4.70 us | 2.29 us |
| `tstp_map` update | 0.61 us | 1.56 us |
| fold over the new table | 3.29 us | 0.47 us |
| new `a2_map` entry | 0.62 us | 0.68 us |
| `a1_map` update | 0.32 us | 0.32 us |
| total (`add_new_mmuaux`) | 10.7 us | 4.2 us |
| imperative window | 0.24 us | 0.09 us |

So an imperative implementation is about 10x faster on the structures themselves. The cost
of the functional ones is not in any single place: it is the persistent red-black-tree
operations, about 0.3 us each on tuple keys (a lookup in a 5000-key tree takes 260-280 ns)
and about 0.6 us each on the time-point-keyed mappings of the Until structure, which hold
one entry per time point in the window (10000 here, because a time stamp spans 1700 time
points); a tuple's lifetime in the Since structure costs about seven such operations, a
time point in the Until structure about eight plus the `eval_step`. The imperative version
does the same in O(1) hash-table and queue operations without allocation.

**What it would gain end to end.** The Since structure is about a quarter of P3 and the
Until structure about 85% of P2 (P2 minus its non-temporal part is 2.0 s of 2.36 s), so the
refinement would bring P3 to about 1.0 s (1.3x) and P2 to about 0.6 s (4x, within 1.5x of
MonPoly); `since`/`once`/`eventually`/`until` would gain 1.5-2x.

**What blocks it.** Imperative HOL programs keep their state on a heap that exists only
inside a `Heap` computation. The auxiliary structures are threaded through `meval` as pure
values (`eval_since` in `Monitor.thy` calls `add_new_ts_msaux`, `join_msaux`,
`add_new_table_msaux`, `result_msaux` on a value of the locale's abstract type, and the
front end calls the pure `mstep` once per time point). A structure that lives on the heap
cannot persist between two pure calls: `run`-style wrappers (as used for the probe join,
Section 8) start from a fresh heap and are sound only for computations whose result is a
value. Making the structure imperative therefore means making the interface monadic: a
locale `maux_imp` with `Heap`-typed operations, a monadic `meval_imp` (the Since/Until cases
and the buffer machinery bind, everything else lifts), imperative representations of
`mmsaux`/`mmuaux` (hash maps for `tuple_in`/`tuple_since`, arrays or deques indexed by time
point for `a2_map`/`tstp_map`) with separation-logic invariants that imply `valid_mmsaux`/
`valid_mmuaux`, and a simulation proof against the pure `meval`. The call sites are few
(`eval_since`, `eval_until`, `minit0`), but the invariants of `Optimized_Since.thy` (1352
lines) and `Optimized_Until.thy` (2286 lines) would have to be re-established for the new
representations. This is weeks of proof work, not something for this branch.

**What was tried instead, and measured negative.**

* Monomorphising the last polymorphic call on the Since path (`add_new_ts_mmsaux'`, called by
  the aggregation wrapper) and boxing the dictionaries of the Until fold: no change; the
  dictionaries are not the cost.
* Fusing the three passes of the window move into one fold: `add_new_ts_mmsaux'` 2.34 us ->
  2.01 us dense, unchanged sparse; nothing end to end (Section 7g).
* A direct tuple comparator inside the tree operations: Containers' abstract tree type does
  accept a type-specific constructor (`lift_definition ed_rbt_insert ... is rbt_comp_insert
  ed_comparator` is accepted by the code generator, contrary to the note in Section 7), but
  a lookup with the direct comparator is only 8% faster than with the generic
  `comparator_list (comparator_option comparator_event_data)` chain: the tree walk and the
  big-integer comparisons dominate, not the closures.

**Cheaper functional alternatives for the Until structure.** Its time-point-keyed mappings
are indexed by consecutive integers; a deque of inner mappings (O(1) at both ends, which is
where new time points arrive and old ones expire) removes most of the 0.6 us tree operations
per step without leaving pure HOL. Done in Section 11, with a simulation relation instead of
redoing the `Optimized_Until` invariants.

## 11. Until structure with deques (implemented)

The "cheaper functional alternative" of Section 10, done as a verified refinement in pure
HOL (`thys/MFODL_Monitor_Devel/Optimized_Until_Deque.thy`, 950 lines).

**The change.** The Until structure `mmuaux` keeps two mappings keyed by the time points of
the window: `a2_map` (for every time point of the window plus the next one, the mapping from
tuples to the time stamp or time point at which they can be output) and `tstp_map` (the time
stamp of every time point). Their key sets are always the contiguous ranges
`{tp - len..tp}` and `{tp - len..<tp}`: `eval_step` removes the smallest key and `add_new`
adds the largest one, and the only other access is the update of arbitrary entries in the
window when a new table arrives. Both are now deques (`'a dq`: a front list, a back list and
their cached lengths, with `dq_tl`, `dq_push`, `dq_nth` and `dq_upd`), so that the per-step
operations of the structure at the two ends of the window are O(1) list operations instead
of persistent red-black-tree operations on big-integer keys (about 0.6 us each, Section 10).
Access to an entry in the middle of the window costs a list walk from the nearer end; the
tree costs O(log n). The entries accessed by `add_new` are the time points at which the
left operand last held for a tuple, clipped to the window: for `EVENTUALLY` and formulas
whose left operand holds at most time points this is the newest entry, for a left operand
that never holds it is the oldest one, both O(1).

**The proof.** Rather than redoing the 2300 lines of `Optimized_Until` invariants for the
new representation, the deque structure `mmuaux_dq` is related to the original `mmuaux` by a
simulation relation `rel_uaux`: all components but the two mappings are equal, and
`a2_map`/`tstp_map` are the mappings whose key sets are the two ranges and whose entry at
`tp - len + k` is the `k`-th deque element. Every operation preserves the relation
(`eval_step_sim`, `shift_sim`, `eval_sim`, `init_sim`, `length_sim`, `add_new_sim`), given
that the original structure is valid (validity supplies `len <= tp`, the key sets and the
bound `tp' < tp` on the values of `a1_map`, which puts all updated entries inside the
window). The abstract type of the `muaux` locale becomes a sum type `muaux_dq` (deque
structure for formulas without aggregation, the aggregation structure `mmauaux` otherwise)
with validity "some valid `mmuaux` is related to the deque structure"; the four locale
lemmas follow from the simulation lemmas and the original locale lemmas, and the
`default_maux` interpretation in `Monitor_Impl` now uses `muaux_dq`. The relation is
functional (`rel_uaux_det`: the related `mmuaux` is determined by the deque structure) and
total on valid structures (`rel_uaux_of`), which also gives the fallback for infinite
operand tables, where the fold below is not available: convert, run `add_new_mmuaux`,
convert back.

**The code.** `add_new_dq` is one fold over the sorted list of the new table
(`csorted_list_of_set`, linear on the tree): each tuple contributes one or two (time point,
tuple) pairs, each pair that passes the interval check goes into the table appended to the
queue, into the deque entry of its time point (`upd_nested_step_dq`: `dq_nth`, update of the
inner mapping, `dq_upd`) and, for the oldest time point, into the result. Folding over the
list rather than the set (as the fused code equation of Section 7g does) needs no
commutativity argument: the deque produced by the fold is characterised entry-wise
(`dq_nth_upd_nested_dq`), which is all the simulation needs. The definition is executable
as it stands; only `eval_step_dq` gets a code equation in `Code_Opt.thy` (the union of the
expired tables as a fold, as for `eval_step_mmuaux`).

**Two representation bugs found by measuring.** The first version reversed the whole back
list into the front when the front was exhausted; afterwards every access to the newest
entries walked the whole front list, and P2 got slower (2.15 s against 1.91 s). Moving only
the older half restored the newest entries to the head of the back list, but P2 gained only
5%, and the micro-benchmark (`evaluation/since_bench/until_dq_bench.ml`, EVENTUALLY[0,6),
one tuple per time point, 1700 time points per time stamp) showed why: `add_new` cost 58 us
per step against 9.5 us for the mapping version, all of it in the fold, and the entry the
fold touched was always the oldest one (index 0: for `TRUE UNTIL B` the unit tuple entered
`a1_map` at time point 0 and stays there, so the pair's time point is clipped to the start
of the window), while the front list was empty in 8.5% of the steps: a burst of
`eval_step`s that ends exactly when the front runs out leaves the front empty for the whole
next time stamp, and index 0 is then the far end of the back list (about 10000 elements
walked and copied per step). The final version keeps the front non-empty for every
non-empty deque (`dq_tl` normalises after removing the head, `dq_push` builds the front when
it is empty), so both ends are O(1) always.

**Results.** Micro-benchmark (`evaluation/since_bench/until_dq_bench.ml`, same setting as
Section 10), per step:

| operation | mapping version, dense | deque, dense | mapping, sparse | deque, sparse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `shift` (runs the `eval_step`s) | 4.70 us | 4.07 us | 2.29 us | 1.44 us |
| `tstp_map` update / `tstp_dq` push | 0.61 us | 0.05 us | 1.56 us | 0.05 us |
| fold over the new table | 3.29 us | 2.51 us | 0.47 us | 0.33 us |
| new `a2_map` entry / `a2_dq` push | 0.62 us | 0.04 us | 0.68 us | 0.04 us |
| `a1_map` update | 0.32 us | 0.42 us | 0.32 us | 0.42 us |
| `add_new` total | 10.2 us | 6.9 us | 4.5 us | 2.3 us |

The four tree operations per step on time-point keys are gone (1.5x dense, 1.9x sparse on
the structure); what remains is the `eval_step` (`Mapping.combine` of the expiring inner
mapping into the next one, the `to_del` filter) and the tuple-keyed inner mappings.

cram tests pass):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2 | 0.41 s | 1.71 s | 1.18 s | 1.45x | 2.9x |
| P3 | 0.42 s | 1.24 s | 1.23 s | 1.01x | 2.9x |

(`and`, `andnot`, `since`, `agg`: unchanged within noise.) P2, whose window spans about
10000 time points, gains 1.45x and is now within 2.9x of MonPoly (3.9x before this
branch's Section 7g, 8.6x at the start). The synthetic `eventually`/`until` benchmarks do
not move: their windows hold at most a few dozen time points, where a red-black tree on
small integers is already cheap. The remaining cost of P2 is the `eval_step` and the
tuple-keyed operations, i.e. the constant factors of Section 2.

**Trade-off.** The deque makes both ends O(1) but an entry in the middle of the window
costs a list walk from the nearer end, where the tree costs O(log n). The entries touched
by `add_new` are the time points at which the left operand last held for the tuple, clipped
to the window: the oldest entry when the left operand is a constant or rarely true, the
newest ones when it holds continuously. A left operand that changes truth value every few
hundred time points inside a window of thousands would hit the middle; a balanced
random-access sequence (a Braun tree, `HOL-Data_Structures`) would give O(log n) middle
access with cheap index arithmetic instead of comparisons, at the price of O(log n) ends.
