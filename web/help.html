2.0 Help

VeriMon is a monitor for metric first-order dynamic logic (MFODL). It checks a log, a sequence of time-stamped sets of events, against a formula, and reports at which time points and for which values of the formula's free variables the formula is satisfied. VeriMon 2.0 is formally verified end to end in Isabelle/HOL: the parsers of formulas, signatures and logs, the preparation of the formula and the monitor are code generated from the formalization. This page runs that code, compiled to JavaScript, in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

The sources, the formalization, the command-line tool and its documentation are at github.com/dtraytel/verimon.

Using this page

  • Examples: pick one from the list, or edit any of the three inputs; each can also be loaded from a file.
  • Signature: the predicates that the formula and the log may use, with their argument types (syntax).
  • MFODL formula: the property to check (syntax).
  • negate: monitor the negation of the formula. A policy is usually written as what must hold, and its violations are the satisfactions of its negation.
  • Log: the events (syntax).
  • Verdicts: the satisfactions, one line per time point (format).

The inputs are checked as you type. A green border means the input is fine. A red border means an error; hover over the input to see the message. A yellow formula with a telescope is well-formed but not monitorable; hover over it to see why. A yellow log has verdicts; a check mark on the log means that there are none.

Formulas

Formulas use MonPoly's syntax. In the grammar below, [ ] encloses optional parts, { } parts that may repeat, and | separates alternatives. Keywords are in capitals.

formula ::= TRUE | FALSE
          | pred ( [arg {, arg}] )
          | term = term | term < term | term <= term | term > term | term >= term
          | NOT formula
          | formula AND formula | formula OR formula
          | formula IMPLIES formula | formula EQUIV formula
          | EXISTS var {, var} . formula | FORALL var {, var} . formula
          | PREV [interval] formula | NEXT [interval] formula
          | ONCE [interval] formula | EVENTUALLY [interval] formula
          | PAST_ALWAYS [interval] formula | ALWAYS [interval] formula
          | formula SINCE [interval] formula | formula UNTIL [interval] formula
          | formula TRIGGER [interval] formula | formula RELEASE [interval] formula
          | MATCHP [interval] regex | MATCHF [interval] regex
          | var <- aggop var [; var {, var}] formula
          | LET pred ( [var {, var}] ) = formula IN formula
          | LETPAST pred ( [var {, var}] ) = formula IN formula
          | FRZ pred ( [var {, var}] ) = formula IN formula
          | ( formula )
arg      ::= term | _

term     ::= var | constant
          | term + term | term - term | term * term | term / term | term MOD term | - term
          | function ( term ) | ( term )
function ::= i2f | f2i
constant ::= integer | float | "string"          e.g. 42, -7, 3.25, "db2"
aggop    ::= CNT | SUM | AVG | MIN | MAX | MED

interval ::= [bound,bound] | (bound,bound] | [bound,bound) | (bound,bound)
bound    ::= integer [d | h | m | s] | *        e.g. [0,7d], (0,10], [1,*)

regex    ::= regex + regex                        alternative
          | regex regex                           concatenation
          | regex *                               repetition
          | .                                     one step (wildcard)
          | formula ?                             test
          | formula                               a test and a step
          | ( regex )

An argument _ of an event is a variable that occurs nowhere else. The bounds of an interval are distances between timestamps: a plain number is in the log's timestamp units, and days (d), hours (h), minutes (m) and seconds (s) assume that timestamps are seconds; an upper bound * is unbounded. Variables and predicate names consist of letters, digits and _, and start with a letter. A variable that is not bound by a quantifier, an aggregation or a LET is a free variable: the verdicts list its values. (* ... *) and # to the end of the line are comments.

Precedence

bindsoperators
tightestNOT
AND (left associative)
OR (left associative)
IMPLIES (right associative)
EQUIV (left associative)
loosestSINCE, UNTIL, TRIGGER, RELEASE (right associative)

The prefix operators extend as far to the right as possible: PREV, NEXT, ONCE, EVENTUALLY, PAST_ALWAYS, ALWAYS, EXISTS and FORALL include the Boolean connectives to their right but stop at a binary temporal operator, so ONCE p(x) AND q(x) SINCE r(x) is (ONCE (p(x) AND q(x))) SINCE r(x). LET, LETPAST, FRZ and aggregations include everything to their right. When in doubt, add parentheses.

Meaning

For a formula φ with subformulas α and β, an interval I and a regular expression r:

φφ holds at time point i if
PREV I αα holds at i−1, and the distance of their timestamps is in I
NEXT I αα holds at i+1, within distance I
ONCE I αα holds at some earlier or the same time point within distance I
EVENTUALLY I αα holds at some later or the same time point within distance I
PAST_ALWAYS I αα holds at all earlier and the same time points within distance I
ALWAYS I αα holds at all later and the same time points within distance I
α SINCE I ββ holds at some time point j ≤ i within distance I, and α at all time points after j up to i
α UNTIL I ββ holds at some time point j ≥ i within distance I, and α at all time points from i before j
α TRIGGER I βat every earlier or the same time point j within distance I, β holds or α held at some time point after j (the dual of SINCE)
α RELEASE I βthe future dual of TRIGGER
MATCHP I rsome stretch of time points ending at i and starting within distance I matches r
MATCHF I rsome stretch of time points starting at i and ending within distance I matches r
y <- SUM x; g αy is the sum of x over the satisfactions of α, grouped by the values of g
LET p(x) = α IN ββ holds, where p stands for α
LETPAST p(x) = α IN βas LET, but α may refer to p at earlier time points (recursion through PREV, ONCE, ...)
FRZ p(x) = α IN ββ holds, where p stands for the satisfactions of α at i, at every time point β refers to

The distance is the difference of the timestamps; an interval without bound is [0,*). In a regular expression, α? tests α without moving and . moves one time point. A bare formula α is a test and a step: α? . in MATCHF, . α? in MATCHP. Some keywords have synonyms: PREVIOUS, SOMETIMES (for EVENTUALLY), HISTORICALLY (for PAST_ALWAYS), and |> or FORWARD (for MATCHF), <| or BACKWARD (for MATCHP).

Monitorability

VeriMon computes the finite set of satisfying values at each time point. This requires the formula to be monitorable: roughly, every free variable must be bound by an event or an equation, a negation may only follow a conjunct that binds its variables (p(x) AND NOT q(x), not NOT q(x) alone), the two sides of OR must have the same free variables, and future operators need bounded intervals. VeriMon rewrites a formula that is not monitorable as it stands, for example by pushing negations inwards; if that does not help, the formula is marked with the telescope. A policy p(x) IMPLIES ONCE q(x) is not monitorable, but its negation is: tick negate to see its violations.

MonPoly's SUBSTRING, MATCHES, regular expression constants r"..." and its other term functions (i2s, YEAR, FORMAT_DATE, ...) are parsed but not supported.

Signatures

One predicate per line, with the types of its arguments, optionally named:

publish(int)
approve(manager:string, report:int)
reading(sensor:string, value:float)

The types are int, float and string. The predicates tp(i), ts(t) and tpts(i, t), which give the current time point and timestamp, are built in.

Logs

A log is a sequence of time points. Each starts with @ and its timestamp, a non-negative integer, followed by its events: a predicate name and one or more tuples of values.

@0  approve(Alice, 152) publish(160)
@10 publish(152)(163)            # two events of publish
@10 approve("Bob", 163);         # same timestamp, next time point
@25

Timestamps must not decrease. Strings may be written without quotes if they consist of letters, digits and _[]/:-.!. A ; may end a time point, and # starts a comment. Time units in formulas (7d) assume that timestamps are seconds.

Verdicts

@1307955600 (time point 0): (160)
@1308477599 (time point 1): (152) (187)

Each line gives a timestamp, the time point, and the tuples of values of the free variables for which the formula is satisfied there, in the order in which the variables first occur in the formula. A formula without free variables shows true. A verdict at a time point is shown once it is final: a formula with future operators waits for the time points that decide it.
